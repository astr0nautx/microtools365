Passport & Document Photo Sizes in India: A Practical Guide

Published August 4, 2026 · 5 min read

Almost every Indian application form — passport, exam forms, government IDs, job portals — asks for a photo and signature in a specific size and file format. Get it wrong and the form either rejects the upload outright or flags it for manual review later. Here's how to actually get it right the first time.

Why there's no single universal size

Unlike a lot of countries with one standardized photo spec, Indian applications vary by issuing authority. Passport photos, PAN card photos, various competitive exam forms, and individual job portals each set their own requirements — sometimes measured in millimeters, sometimes in pixels, sometimes just as a file size cap in KB. There genuinely isn't one number that works everywhere, which is exactly why so many people get this wrong and have to redo it.

Common patterns you'll run into

  • Passport-style photos are typically square or near-square, with a plain light background, and are commonly requested somewhere in the 200×230 to 350×350 pixel range depending on the form.
  • Signature uploads are almost always a small, wide rectangle rather than square — commonly cited ranges are around 140×60 pixels, though this varies by application.
  • File size caps are extremely common — many government exam portals cap uploads at 20-50 KB for photos and even smaller for signatures, which usually means you need to compress the image, not just resize it.
  • Format is almost always JPG for photos; PNG occasionally shows up for signatures if a transparent background is expected.

The single most important habit: read the specific form's instructions every time, rather than assuming last year's exam or last application's requirement still applies. Requirements do change between application cycles.

The actual workflow

  1. Take or select a clear, well-lit photo against a plain background.
  2. Check the exact pixel dimensions and file size limit stated on the form you're filling.
  3. Crop to the correct aspect ratio first — this matters more than people expect, since a photo cropped to the wrong ratio and then force-resized will look stretched or squeezed.
  4. Resize to the exact required pixel dimensions.
  5. If there's a file size cap, reduce the JPG quality until it fits comfortably under the limit without looking visibly degraded.

The mistake most people make

The most common error isn't getting the dimensions wrong — it's uploading a huge, high-resolution photo straight from a phone camera without resizing at all, and having the form silently reject it or throw a vague error. The second most common mistake is cropping loosely to "roughly the right shape" instead of the exact aspect ratio the form expects, which distorts the photo once it's force-resized by the portal.
